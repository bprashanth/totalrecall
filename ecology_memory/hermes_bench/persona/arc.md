# Kavya persona and conservation drill-down arc

## Persona

Kavya, 32, is a programme manager at a small conservation NGO in Krishnagiri. She works with
field ecologists, restoration crews and neighbouring villages around Elephants by the Lake. Her
English is direct Indian-English: warm, practical, occasionally “no?” or “itself”. She knows the
site operationally but wants claims she can defend to donors and field teams. She challenges vague
answers, asks what was actually measured, and distinguishes a useful estimate from invented certainty.

## Arc

1. OPEN — map EBTL for a new conservation colleague: strongest local facts and major unknowns.
2. WILDLIFE BASELINE — what groups were actually surveyed, when, and what “recorded” means.
3. BIODIVERSITY COVERAGE — what important groups or seasons are missing; why inventory is not richness.
4. INVASIVES — what is locally documented versus public-bbox or regional evidence.
5. SCARCE-1: INVASIVE REBOUND — whether Lantana or other invasives are returning after removal; expect unknown + concrete repeated-map DATA REQUEST.
6. FIRE — observed fire history versus present/future risk; fuels/weather/field gaps.
7. ASSISTED NATURAL REGENERATION — evidence for choosing ANR versus planting across the property; avoid recommendation without treatment/plot data.
8. NURSERY + PHYLOGENY — what native material is documented and whether phylogenetic/functional diversity can be assessed.
9. SNAKES — locally documented venomous/non-venomous species and survey-status boundary.
10. ARACHNIDS — sparse local data, regional discovery, and environmental transfer gates.
11. HABITAT PROTECTION — which measured habitat features would support snake/arachnid protection; refuse invented tree dependencies.
12. EASTERN GHATS TRANSFER — how to use surrounding regional records without presenting them as EBTL observations.
13. SCARCE-2: COMMUNITIES + LIVELIHOODS — grazing, fuelwood, restoration jobs, crop loss or coexistence around the site; expect unknown + ethical household/usage DATA REQUEST.
14. SYNTHESIS — a short field-team brief: defensible findings, bounded estimates, immediate conservation actions, and the three highest-value data collections.

## Follow-up generation

One grinder model writes the next shared question after seeing all arms' previous answers. It must
refer only to a point common to the answers, challenge or drill into it, and ask the current arc
goal in 1–2 sentences. It supplies no new ecological facts.
