The **biggest doubt** is whether habitat at **EBTL itself** is suitable for king cobra, not just whether the wider region is. Public sources suggest the larger **Bilgiri-Melagiri / Eastern Ghats landscape** can support the species, but I did **not** find a verified public record from inside EBTL or its immediate surroundings. Sources: [EBTL](https://ebtl.earth/), [Ganesh et al. 2018](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331523059_Herpetofauna_of_Southern_Eastern_Ghats_India-II_From_Western_Ghats_to_Coromandel_Coast), [India Biodiversity Portal](https://indiabiodiversity.org/species/show/238894).

What the field team should check at EBTL:
- Look for **good king cobra habitat**: moist forest patches, shaded stream lines, dense cover, low disturbance.
- Check whether there are **many other snakes**, especially rat snakes and other forest snakes, because king cobras feed mainly on snakes.
- Ask for and verify any **direct evidence**: photos, shed skins, roadkills, rescue records, camera-trap bycatch, or reliable sightings from staff and nearby communities.
- Search likely areas in the **wet season and early morning**, especially along streams, forest edges, and less-disturbed slopes.

What I found:
- The region is connected to a larger forested hill system, so presence is **possible**.
- I found **no public confirmed record inside EBTL**.

What I estimate:
- If EBTL still has suitable moist forest and enough prey snakes, king cobra presence is **possible but probably rare**.

What is missing:
- A **verified EBTL record**
- **Georeferenced nearby records**
- A **systematic local survey** focused on large forest snakes

A simple NGO-safe answer would be: **“The main uncertainty is whether EBTL itself has the right habitat and prey base. Our field team should first check moist forest patches, stream lines, snake abundance, and any direct local evidence.”**
