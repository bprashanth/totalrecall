Yes, they still could be there, but I would treat it as **possible and probably rare**, not likely/common.

What I found:
- EBTL is in the **Krishnagiri-Melagiri foothill landscape** in Tamil Nadu, not an isolated site. Public EBTL pages place it near Budhimutlu in the eastern edge of the **Bilgiri-Melagiri hill complex**. Source: [EBTL site](https://ebtl.earth/), [research page](https://ebtl.earth/cegs/).
- A 2018 public herpetofauna survey of the **Bilgiri-Melagiri massif and other Southern Eastern Ghats sites** recorded 37 snake species, but I did **not** find king cobra listed in that survey. Source: [Ganesh et al. 2018](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331523059_Herpetofauna_of_Southern_Eastern_Ghats_India-II_From_Western_Ghats_to_Coromandel_Coast).
- Public citizen-science checklists I found for **Krishnagiri** and **Melagiris** also did **not** show king cobra records. Sources: [Krishnagiri checklist](https://www.inaturalist.org/check_lists/316682-Krishnagiri-Check-List), [Melagiris checklist](https://www.inaturalist.org/check_lists/4104133-Melagiris-Check-List).
- But king cobra is publicly recorded from **Tamil Nadu** and from the **Eastern Ghats more broadly**. Sources: [India Biodiversity Portal](https://indiabiodiversity.org/species/show/238894), [Eastern Ghats Wildlife Society](https://eghats.org/home/program/king-cobra-conservation), [Indian Reptiles](https://www.indianreptiles.org/ophiophagus-hannah).

My estimate:
- **Inside EBTL itself:** possible, but I would guess **low probability at present**.
- **In the wider nearby hill forest landscape:** more plausible, especially in **better forested, wetter, less disturbed patches** than the EBTL valley floor.
- So the most careful wording is: **“No confirmed public record inside EBTL, but occasional presence in the surrounding landscape is still biologically plausible.”**
