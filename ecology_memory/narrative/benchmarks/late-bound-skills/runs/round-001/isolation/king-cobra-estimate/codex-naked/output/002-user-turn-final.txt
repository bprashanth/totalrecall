I would **accept the estimate only in a cautious form**:

**Accepted:** “King cobras could still occur in or near EBTL, but probably rarely.”
**Rejected:** “King cobras are likely/common in EBTL” or “they are definitely absent.”

Why I accepted the cautious estimate:
- **EBTL is connected to a larger hill-forest landscape**, not an isolated site, so absence of records does not prove absence. Source: [EBTL](https://ebtl.earth/), [CEGS at EBTL](https://ebtl.earth/cegs/).
- **King cobra is known from Tamil Nadu and the Eastern Ghats region**, so the species is not impossible there. Sources: [India Biodiversity Portal](https://indiabiodiversity.org/species/show/238894), [Eastern Ghats Wildlife Society](https://eghats.org/home/program/king-cobra-conservation), [Indian Reptiles](https://www.indianreptiles.org/ophiophagus-hannah).
- **No record inside EBTL is not strong evidence of absence**, especially for a rare, hard-to-detect snake.

Why I rejected a stronger estimate:
- A public **Southern Eastern Ghats herpetofauna survey** did not clearly show king cobra from the Melagiri-side area I checked. Source: [Ganesh et al. 2018](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331523059_Herpetofauna_of_Southern_Eastern_Ghats_India-II_From_Western_Ghats_to_Coromandel_Coast).
- Public **Krishnagiri** and **Melagiris** checklists I found also did not show king cobra records. Sources: [Krishnagiri checklist](https://www.inaturalist.org/check_lists/316682-Krishnagiri-Check-List), [Melagiris checklist](https://www.inaturalist.org/check_lists/4104133-Melagiris-Check-List).
- So there is **not enough public evidence** to say they are definitely present in EBTL.

What is missing:
- A verified **record from inside EBTL**
- **Georeferenced nearby records** from the immediate EBTL surroundings
- A **systematic local survey** or rescue log

So, in simple terms: I accepted a **possible but rare** estimate because the wider landscape makes presence plausible, and I rejected any **stronger claim** because the public local evidence is still weak.
