Q: We have Rs 2 lakh budget for data collection in Erode district to understand livelihoods. What should we measure, where, and what can we estimate from existing data instead? Give citations for whatever data you say already exists.
elapsed:82s

---

To design an effective livelihood study in **Erode district, Tamil Nadu** within a tight budget of **Rs. 2 lakh (INR 200,000)**, you must adopt a **mixed-methods, strategic-sampling approach**. Rather than duplicating existing macro-level data, your budget should be reserved for high-resolution primary data collection on informal wages, volatility, agricultural value chains, and vulnerability.

---

### I. What Can We Estimate from Existing Data? (Save the Budget)
Do not spend your Rs. 2 lakh budget collecting basic demographic or macro-economic indicators. The following data already exists and can be extracted to establish baseline profiles:

1. **Broad Work Classification and Demographics**
   - **What already exists:** Total population, sex ratio, literacy, and counts of Main/Marginal workers categorized as Cultivators, Agricultural Labourers, Household Industry (weaving/coir) workers, and Other workers at the village, block, and taluk level.
   - **Citation:** *Census of India 2011: Primary Census Abstract (PCA) for Erode District*. Tamil Nadu Directorate of Census Operations. Available via [Erode District Administration Portal](https://erode.nic.in/documents/census/).
2. **Household Assets, Sanitation, and Basic Infrastructure**
   - **What already exists:** Household ownership of assets (electricity, clean drinking water, sanitation facilities, bank accounts, mobile phones, internet, and health insurance) and maternal/child nutrition indicators.
   - **Citation:** *National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5), 2019-21: Tamil Nadu - Erode District Factsheet*. International Institute for Population Sciences (IIPS) and ICF, 2021. Available via [IIPS RCH Portal](https://rchiips.org/nfhs/districtfactsheet_NFHS-5.shtml).
3. **Landholding Sizes and Distribution**
   - **What already exists:** Size distribution of operational landholdings (marginal, small, semi-medium, medium, large). In Erode, the average holding size is **1.65 hectares**, with small/marginal holdings (<2 ha) constituting **74.33%** of all holdings but covering only **38.46%** of the land.
   - **Citation:** *Tenth Agricultural Census (2015-16): Phase-II Report for Tamil Nadu*. Department of Economics and Statistics, Government of Tamil Nadu (published 2024); and *Erode District Agriculture Plan (DAP)*, Tamil Nadu Agricultural University (TNAU).
4. **Industrial & Textile Clusters**
   - **What already exists:** Counts of micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs). Erode has **191 Handloom Weavers’ Cooperative Societies** and **57 Powerloom Weavers’ Cooperative Societies**, with major readymade garment clusters concentrated in Erode, Bhavani, and Chennimalai, and a Coir Cluster in Nathakadaiyur.
   - **Citation:** *Brief Industrial Profile of Erode District*, Development Commissioner, Ministry of MSME, Government of India; and *Department of Handlooms*, Erode District Administration. Available via [Erode Handlooms](https://erode.nic.in/handlooms-erode/).

### II. What Should We Measure? (The Primary Survey)
With macro baselines established, use your primary survey to capture **micro-level dynamics and vulnerabilities** that government databases miss:

1. **Wages & Income Volatility:**
   - Actual daily/piece-rate wages of informal workers (especially powerloom/handloom weavers and agricultural laborers).
   - Underemployment: The average number of days of employment available per year (highly volatile in the textile sector due to export fluctuations and power tariff changes).
2. **Agricultural Value Chain & Turmeric Economics:**
   - Erode is the second-largest turmeric market in India and has a Geographical Indication (GI) tag for its high-curcumin turmeric. 
   - Measure actual cost of cultivation vs. price realized at the cooperative markets (Erode, Gobichettipalayam, and Perundurai), including middle-man margins and transport costs.
3. **Household Debt & Credit Traps:**
   - Percentage of credit sourced from formal banks/cooperatives vs. high-interest local informal moneylenders. 
   - Debt service ratio: The proportion of monthly household income spent on debt repayment.
4. **Occupational Hazards and Health Expenditures:**
   - Health out-of-pocket expenses, which are disproportionately high in Erode due to chemical exposure in dye houses (Bhavani/Chennimalai) and heavy pesticide use in cash-crop zones.

### III. Where Should We Measure? (Geographic Strategy)
Given your budget, aim for a sample size of **300 households**. You should use a **Stratified Purposive Sampling design** dividing Erode into **3 distinct Livelihood Archetypes** (100 households per zone, clustered in 2–3 nearby villages):

```
                                  Erode District Livelihoods
                                              |
      +---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
      |                                       |                                       |
1. Textile/Weaving Belt              2. High-Value cash Crops                3. Tribal/Rainfed Forest Fringe
(Bhavani & Chennimalai Blocks)     (Gobichettipalayam & Kodumudi Blocks)             (Talavadi Block)
   • 100 households                     • 100 households                         • 100 households
   • Handloom & Powerloom weavers      • Turmeric, sugarcane, banana cultivators    • Scheduled Tribes (ST)
   • Focus: Earnings volatility         • Focus: Cost vs. market returns         • Focus: Forest produce & rainfed crops
```

1. **Zone 1: The Textile and Weaving Belt (Bhavani & Chennimalai Blocks)**
   - *Why:* Bhavani is a globally recognized carpet weaving hub (Bhavani Jamakkalam GI tag) and Chennimalai is a major handloom cluster.
   - *Focus:* Weavers' earnings, shift from handloom to powerloom, piece-rate wage fairness, and occupational health.
2. **Zone 2: The High-Value Cash Crop Belt (Gobichettipalayam & Kodumudi Blocks)**
   - *Why:* Gobi is highly fertile, irrigated by the Bhavanisagar and Kaveri canal systems, and is the agricultural engine of the district.
   - *Focus:* Smallholder turmeric and sugarcane economics, agricultural tenancy, and labor shortages.
3. **Zone 3: The Tribal/Rainfed Forest Fringe (Talavadi Block)**
   - *Why:* Located in the Western Ghats (Sathyamangalam Tiger Reserve foothills), Thalavadi has the highest tribal (ST) population density in the district.
   - *Focus:* Collection of non-timber forest produce (NTFP), rainfed maize/tapioca farming, and dependency on MGNREGA.
