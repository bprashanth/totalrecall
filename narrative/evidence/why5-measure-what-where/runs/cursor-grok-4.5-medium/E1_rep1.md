# E1 rep1 x cursor-grok-4.5-medium
Q: We have Rs 2 lakh budget for data collection in Erode district to understand livelihoods. What should we measure, where, and what can we estimate from existing data instead? Give citations for whatever data you say already exists.
elapsed:100s

---

**Verdict:** With ₹2 lakh, do not run a district-representative labour-force survey. Use existing public data for *who works where*, and spend the budget on a **stratified livelihood-systems study** that fills gaps official data never measure well: earnings, seasonality, debt, and how households combine farm / loom / wage / migration work.

## What you *should* measure (only with primary data)

Official sources are weak on continuous **income**, **piece rates**, **multi-job portfolios**, **debt terms**, and **intra-HH bargaining**. Prioritise a short module (~25–35 minutes):

1. **Livelihood portfolio (last 12 months)** — all earners: primary + secondary work; months active; farm / loom / dyeing / construction / trade / domestic / MGNREGA / migration.
2. **Earnings & terms** — cash wage or piece rate; who pays (jobber / master weaver / contractor); delayed payment; unpaid family work hours.
3. **Seasonality & shocks** — lean months; crop failure / yarn price / power cuts / health; coping (debt, asset sale, migrate, cut meals).
4. **Assets & claims that affect livelihoods** — land operated vs owned; loom ownership vs rental; livestock sold for cash; SHG / bank / moneylender debt and interest.
5. **Scheme access quality** — not “heard of X”, but last use of MGNREGA, crop insurance, weaver welfare, e-Shram, PDS linkage to work gaps.
6. **Mobility** — who migrated, where (Tiruppur / Coimbatore / Chennai / Gulf), remittances, who stayed behind.

Add **8–12 FGDs** and **20–30 key informants** (jobbers, commission agents, co-op secretaries, VAOs, SHG leaders, tannery/dyeing supervisors) — that is where value-chain margins and labour contracting become clear.

Skip long consumption modules (NSS-style) and anthropometry (NFHS already covers health/nutrition).

## Where (sampling under ₹2 lakh)

Erode is not one livelihood system. Stratify into **4–5 livelihood zones**, then pick **2–3 clusters each** (village or urban block), **20–25 households per cluster**.

| Zone | Where to go | Why |
|------|-------------|-----|
| Powerloom / textile wage | Erode city wards; Chennimalai; Bhavani–Anthiyur belt | Dominant “other workers” + co-op / mega-cluster economy ([Handlooms Dept](https://erode.nic.in/handlooms-erode/); [TexMin mega cluster](https://www.texmin.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/12/c865d599cae0c357c02d247a8a82d24e.pdf)) |
| Commercial irrigated farming | Gobi, Modakurichi, Kodumudi / similar canal–well tracts | Turmeric, banana, sugarcane, coconut systems in DAP/handbook |
| Land-poor / ag-labour + NREGA | High ag-labour Census villages + high MGNREGA person-day GPs from [MIS](https://nregastrep.nic.in/netnrega/homedist.aspx?state_name=TAMIL%20NADU&district_name=ERODE&District_Code=2910&State_Code=29) | Tests whether Census “ag labour” still holds and how lean months are bridged |
| Forest-fringe / ST | Sathyamangalam–Thalavadi side | Different asset base and mobility; often missed in urban-biased samples |
| Peri-urban / industrial spillover | Perundurai / SIPCOT-adjacent settlements | Wage work linked to factories + commuting |

**Realistic n:** ~200–250 HH + qualitative work. At ~₹600–900 all-in per HH (enumerator, travel, supervision, data entry), ₹2 lakh is exhausted. That is enough for **comparisons across zones**, not for precise district % with tight confidence intervals.

Frame clusters using Census village PCA (share of cultivators / ag labour / other workers) + MGNREGA intensity + known loom concentrations — free stratification, paid fieldwork only inside chosen clusters.

## What you can *estimate* from existing data vs survey

| Question | Existing data | Your survey |
|----------|---------------|-------------|
| How many work in agriculture vs non-farm? | Census 2011 (outdated levels; still best spatial map) + PLFS for recent *district* labour rates | Update *quality* of work, not the map |
| Where are landless / deprived rural HH? | SECC | Current coping and debt |
| Holding structure | Ag Census / DAP | Actual operated land, lease, and farm income this year |
| Is NREGA used as livelihood insurance? | MIS person-days by GP | Why households do/don’t demand work; wage vs farm trade-offs |
| Textile employment size | Admin co-op counts; ASI/industry lists (incomplete for informal looms) | Piece rates, jobber dependence, women’s unpaid finishing |
| Welfare / assets / women’s status | NFHS-5 | Link to livelihoods (time poverty, bargaining over earnings) |
| Income, diversification, debt, migration returns | **Weak / absent at district HH level** | **Core survey payoff** |

## Bottom line

Treat Census + SECC + Handbook + MGNREGA MIS + Handloom admin data as your **sampling and context layer**. Spend ₹2 lakh on **earnings, contracts, seasonality, and debt across Erode’s textile, farm, labour, and forest-fringe systems** — that is the livelihood information those sources cannot give you.
