Q: Are the dyeing units in Perundurai side paying better than farm work nearby? Give citations I can check.
elapsed:115s

---

**Often yes for annual take-home and for women/unskilled entry paths — but not always on a raw male peak-day rate.** Nearby farm day wages have risen hard because of industrial labour pull, so the gap is narrower than older studies suggested.

### What the sources say

**1. Regional research (Tiruppur–Perundurai industrial hinterland)**  
Sussex / DFID–ESRC fieldwork concluded garment/textile wages were **generally higher than daily agricultural wages**, though with longer hours and commuting. Industry also pushed farm wages up by tightening rural labour markets.  
- PDF: [Looking beyond the industrial cluster](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a08b20e5274a31e00009ae/60620_Looking_beyond.pdf)

Related ethnography (same belt): factory helper pay can start near farm rates, but skilled piece-rate work rises much higher; farm work is seasonal.  
- [Carswell & De Neve, “T-shirts and Tumblers…”](https://doi.org/10.35648/20.500.12413/11781/ii096)

**2. Dyeing / knitwear pay (current market quotes)**  
Tiruppur wage talks (dyeing & bleaching are in the same cluster agreements as hosiery):  
- Unskilled: about **₹350–400/day**  
- Skilled shift work (cutting, knitting, checking, etc.): about **₹500/day**  
- Piece-rate workers often earn more  
Source: [New Indian Express, 6 Nov 2025](https://www.newindianexpress.com/states/tamil-nadu/2025/Nov/06/tiruppur-knitwear-workers-and-manufacturers-to-strike-new-wage-deal-in-two-months)

A later cluster deal (March 2026) covers **dyeing & bleaching** with a staged hike (about 33% over four years).  
- [Cotton Monk summary of the pact](https://www.cottonmonk.com/blog/historic-33-wage-hike-agreed-for-tiruppur-knitwear-workers/)

Perundurai SIPCOT **supervisor** ads (not shop-floor helpers): about **₹25,000–35,000/month**.  
- Example listing: [Sercanto – Dyeing & Finishing Supervisor, SIPCOT Perundurai](https://www.sercanto.in/detail/a/dyeing-finishing-supervisor_perundurai_244575115)

**3. Nearby farm wages**  
Kangeyam (Tiruppur district, same labour market as Perundurai): farmer-reported **₹700/day for men**, **₹400/day for women**.  
- [The Hindu, 13 Oct 2024](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/a-gender-divide-not-even-policy-could-fix/article68747157.ece)

Broader Tamil Nadu rural survey averages (not Erode-only): ag labour about **₹370/day men**, **₹200/day women**; few workdays per year.  
- [CPI(M) Tamil Nadu rural manual labour survey 2023](https://cpim.org/tamil-nadu-rural-manual-labour-survey-2023/)

**4. Statutory floors (not market pay)**  
- Bleaching/dyeing is a scheduled employment; VDA under G.O. (2D) No. 90 (31 Oct / 23 Nov 2022). DA table: [TN DA statement (Apr 2026)](https://ascent-hr.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Tamil-Nadu-Minimum-Wages-26-27.pdf)  
- Labour Bureau series for TN bleaching & dyeing minimum (e.g. ~₹257.5/day in 2022 on CEIC): [CEIC indicator page](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/india/minimum-daily-wage-rate-minimum-wages-act-1948-tamil-nadu/minimum-daily-wage-rate-tamil-nadu-bleaching-and-dyeing-industry)  
- Ag minimums cited in The Hindu (2021 G.O.): e.g. **₹229** for six hours on harvesting/sowing/weeding — often below actual local market rates.

**5. Official rural wage series to check yourself**  
Labour Bureau “Wage Rates in Rural India” (state/occupation tables):  
- [labourbureau.gov.in/wage-rates-in-rural-india](https://www.labourbureau.gov.in/wage-rates-in-rural-india)  
- [labourbureau.gov.in/rural-wages](https://labourbureau.gov.in/rural-wages)

### Bottom line

| Comparison | Who “pays better”? |
|---|---|
| Male **peak farm day** vs unskilled dyeing/knitwear time rate | Farm can be similar or higher (~₹700 vs ~₹350–500) |
| **Women’s** farm day vs factory | Factory usually better or similar, plus more regular days |
| **Yearly earnings / days of work** | Dyeing usually wins — farm is seasonal |
| Skilled / supervisory dyeing | Clearly above farm |

So: dyeing units on the Perundurai side are still widely treated as the better **livelihood** than nearby farm work (more days, overtime, skill ladders), but a man doing peak-season farm labour nearby may clear a **higher single-day cash wage** than an unskilled dyeing helper on time rate.
