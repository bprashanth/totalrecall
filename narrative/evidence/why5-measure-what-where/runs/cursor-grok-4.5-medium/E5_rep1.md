# E5 rep1 x cursor-grok-4.5-medium
Q: Our NGO can survey only 300 households in Erode district this year. Which blocks should we go to and what should we ask, if the goal is understanding how farm families are coping? Citations for any data you use.
elapsed:79s

---

**Go to 6 blocks that represent distinct farming systems, not a thin spread across all 14.** With 300 households, that gives ~50 interviews per block—enough to compare coping patterns without drowning in logistics.

Erode has **14 panchayat union blocks** and a net cultivated area of about **1.57 lakh ha**, with major crops including paddy (~25,100 ha), millets (~20,100 ha), oilseeds (~22,900 ha), and sugarcane (~16,000 ha); about **70% of crops are irrigated**, mainly from Lower Bhavani, Thadapalli–Arakkankottai, Kalingarayan, and Mettur West Bank canals ([Erode District Agriculture Department](https://erode.nic.in/departments/agriculture/); [Development Administration – 14 blocks](https://erode.nic.in/development-administration/)).

---

## Which blocks (and why)

| Block | Livelihood type you capture | Evidence for including it |
|---|---|---|
| **Kodumudi** | Canal + turmeric / commercial crops | Turmeric growers report price swings, storage gaps, credit stress, and labour shortages ([JSRR 2024](https://doi.org/10.9734/jsrr/2024/v30i92367)); sugarcane common in canal areas of Kodumudi ([Erode DAP 2017–18](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/govt_schemes_services/pdf/NADP/6.%20Erode%20DAP%20Final%20Report%202017-18.pdf)) |
| **Bhavani** | Canal paddy–sugarcane systems | Canal cropping (paddy–gingelly, sugarcane) and high cropping intensity in the DAP block profiles ([Erode DAP](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/govt_schemes_services/pdf/NADP/6.%20Erode%20DAP%20Final%20Report%202017-18.pdf)) |
| **Nambiyur** | Garden land + **over-exploited groundwater** | Listed as groundwater **over-exploited**; garden-land sugarcane/groundnut systems ([Erode DAP](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/govt_schemes_services/pdf/NADP/6.%20Erode%20DAP%20Final%20Report%202017-18.pdf)); used as an Erode groundnut survey block ([Scientific Reports 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-08573-3)) |
| **Anthiyur** | Rainfed / mixed dryland + GW stress | Rainfed patterns (ragi–pulses–maize); Ammapettai/Anthiyur/Nambiyur are the over-exploited GW set ([Erode DAP](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/govt_schemes_services/pdf/NADP/6.%20Erode%20DAP%20Final%20Report%202017-18.pdf)); tribal millet scheme covers Anthiyur ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/Coimbatore/special-scheme-for-tribal-farmers-in-cultivating-millets-in-erode/article69896872.ece)) |
| **Thalavadi (Talavadi)** | Hill / tribal / millet–livestock systems | Millet scheme target block; hill livelihoods tied to millets and livestock ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/Coimbatore/special-scheme-for-tribal-farmers-in-cultivating-millets-in-erode/article69896872.ece)); rainfed hill millets historically important ([The Hindu 2021](https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/Coimbatore/plea-to-include-erode-under-siruthaniya-iyakkam-scheme/article36087003.ece)) |
| **Modakurichi** | Intensive garden land / diversified commercial farms | Garden-land sugarcane belt; groundnut survey site with Modakurichi ([Scientific Reports 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-08573-3)); livestock concentration noted in DAP |

**Suggested split:** 6 blocks × **50 households** = 300.  
In each block: **5 villages × 10 farm households**, with quota for marginal/small/medium (and in Thalavadi/Anthiyur, include ST/hill hamlets deliberately).

If you must cut to **5 blocks**, drop Modakurichi and keep Kodumudi + Bhavani + Nambiyur + Anthiyur + Thalavadi—you still cover canal, GW stress, dryland, and hill systems.

## What to ask (coping-focused instrument)

Align the core modules with India’s **Situation Assessment Survey of Agricultural Households** domains—income, expenditure, debt, assets, production, technology, schemes ([PIB on SAS](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2165638); [NSS 77th Round summary](https://ruralindiaonline.org/or/library/resource/situation-assessment-of-agricultural-households-and-land-and-livestock-holdings-of-households-in-rural-india/))—then add an explicit **shocks & coping** block (SAS measures conditions; it does not fully map coping behaviour).

Keep the interview to **35–45 minutes**. Prioritise these modules:

1. **Household & farm profile**  
   Size, caste/tribe, land owned/leased, irrigated vs rainfed share, main crops last two seasons, livestock, who works on/off farm.

2. **Income mix (last 12 months)**  
   Crop net return, livestock, wages/casual labour, remittances, non-farm business. Ask share of income from farming vs other sources (critical for “coping”).

3. **Shocks (last 24 months)** — tick all that apply  
   Drought/failed monsoon turn, canal cut or well failure, pest/disease, price crash, input cost spike, illness/death, livestock loss, wildlife/forest conflict (esp. Thalavadi), labour shortage.

4. **Coping responses after each major shock**  
   Borrowed (formal/informal), sold/pawned assets or livestock, reduced food or skipped meals, took extra wage work, migrated (who, where, how long), changed crop/variety, left land fallow, delayed school fees/medical care, claimed insurance, sought NGO/govt relief.

5. **Water & production stress**  
   Main irrigation source; hours/depth of wells; cost of deepening/diesel/electricity; whether they shifted to less water-intensive crops (district schemes actively push millets/pulses/oilseeds as alternatives — [Erode Agri](https://erode.nic.in/departments/agriculture/)).

6. **Markets & prices**  
   Where sold (mandi, trader, FPC, mill); time to payment; distress sale; storage access—especially relevant for turmeric ([JSRR 2024](https://doi.org/10.9734/jsrr/2024/v30i92367)).

7. **Debt & credit**  
   Outstanding amount, source (bank/PACCS/SHG/moneylender), purpose, repayment difficulty, whether borrowed for consumption after a bad season.

8. **Labour & mobility**  
   Family members who left for work in the last year; seasonal vs permanent; textile/mill/construction destinations common in Kongu region; who stayed to farm.

9. **Food & wellbeing (simple screen)**  
   Months of inadequate food; cutting protein/milk; school dropout risk; health expenditure spikes.

10. **Scheme access (only as coping infrastructure)**  
    Crop insurance (PMFBY notifies maize, groundnut, redgram, ragi, sesame in Kharif; samba paddy and sugarcane in Rabi in Erode — [Erode Agri](https://erode.nic.in/departments/agriculture/)); micro-irrigation; millet/tribal schemes; Kisan credit; whether claim/payout actually helped.

11. **Open wrap (2 minutes)**  
    “What did your family do to get through the hardest season in the last two years?” — code later into the coping typology above.

---

## Practical sampling tips

- **Stratify within villages** by land size (marginal &lt;1 ha / small 1–2 ha / others) so coping differences by farm size are visible—NSS SAS finds indebtedness and income structure vary strongly with landholding ([NSS 77th Round](https://ruralindiaonline.org/or/library/resource/situation-assessment-of-agricultural-households-and-land-and-livestock-holdings-of-households-in-rural-india/)).
- Interview **the person who manages farm decisions**, and note gender of respondent; ask separately whether women took extra wage work or cut their own meals.
- Do **not** oversample near towns only; for Thalavadi/Anthiyur, budget travel time and include interior hamlets.

---

**Bottom line:** Survey **Kodumudi, Bhavani, Nambiyur, Anthiyur, Thalavadi, and Modakurichi** (~50 each), and centre the questionnaire on **shocks → coping actions → income/debt/migration outcomes**, not a generic crop census. That design matches Erode’s real split between canal commercial farming, groundwater-stressed garden lands, and hill millet livelihoods.
