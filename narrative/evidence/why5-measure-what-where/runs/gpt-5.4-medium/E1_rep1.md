Q: We have Rs 2 lakh budget for data collection in Erode district to understand livelihoods. What should we measure, where, and what can we estimate from existing data instead? Give citations for whatever data you say already exists.

With `Rs 2 lakh`, I would **not** try to produce a statistically precise district-wide livelihood estimate for all of Erode. Erode has **14 blocks and 225 village panchayats**, so the budget is better used for a **livelihood-zone study** that compares a few well-chosen contexts rather than a thin sample everywhere ([Erode district administration](https://erode.nic.in/development-administration/)).

## What to measure
Measure only the things that existing data cannot tell you well:

1. **Current income mix at household level**
   Capture the share of income from crop farming, livestock, wage labor, salaried work, petty business, transport, textiles/garments, turmeric trade/processing, remittances, and government transfers. Ask both `last 30 days` and `last 12 months` to handle seasonality.

2. **Employment quality, not just employment status**
   Days worked, months with work shortage, casual vs regular work, local commuting vs migration, earnings volatility, unpaid family work, and whether people are stuck in low-return self-employment.

3. **Farm and livestock economics**
   For households in agriculture: operational land, irrigation access, crops grown, input costs, output sales, labor hiring, mechanization, and net margins. For livestock: herd size, milk/egg/meat sales, feed/vet costs, and whether livestock is a main business or a shock buffer.

4. **Debt, liquidity, and distress**
   Outstanding loans by source, repayment stress, gold loans, informal borrowing, crop loss, health shocks, job loss, and whether households use MGNREGA or migration as coping strategies.

5. **Women’s economic roles**
   Women’s paid work, unpaid work in family enterprise/farm, control over earnings, mobility for work, and childcare constraints. This is usually badly measured in administrative data.

6. **Youth livelihoods and transitions**
   What 18-29 year olds are doing now, skill training taken, aspirations, and barriers to moving into better-paying work.

7. **Market linkages**
   Where produce or labor is sold, price risks, transport constraints, contractor dependence, and payment delays.

8. **Programme friction**
   Not just whether a household is “covered,” but whether it can actually access MGNREGA work, pensions, SHG credit, crop insurance, dairy support, or skill programs.

A good survey for this budget is about **220-260 households**, plus **15-20 key informant interviews** and **6-8 FGDs**.

## Where to measure
Sample by **livelihood zone**, not by equal spread across all blocks.

A practical design is **6 zones x 2 settlements each x about 20 households = 240 households**. Suggested zones:

1. **Irrigated agriculture belt**
   Use `Bhavani`, `Kodumudi`, or `Modakkurichi` to capture canal/river-linked farming and commercial crops. Erode’s agriculture plan notes the importance of the `Cauvery` and `Bhavani` rivers and major crops such as paddy, sugarcane, banana, turmeric, coconut, groundnut, and gingelly ([Erode District Agriculture Plan, TNAU/Govt. of Tamil Nadu](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/govt_schemes_services/pdf/NADP/6.%20Erode%20DAP%20Final%20Report%202017-18.pdf)).

2. **Groundwater-stressed dryland smallholder belt**
   Use `Ammapettai`, `Anthiyur`, `Nambiyur`, or `Perundurai`. The same plan classifies several blocks as over-exploited or stressed for groundwater, which is useful for stratification even if you do not use it as your final “estimate” source ([same source](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/govt_schemes_services/pdf/NADP/6.%20Erode%20DAP%20Final%20Report%202017-18.pdf)).

3. **Livestock-heavy belt**
   Use `Thalavadi`, `Sathyamangalam`, `Nambiyur`, or `Modakkurichi`. The district agriculture plan explicitly identifies these blocks as important for cattle and other livestock concentrations ([same source](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/govt_schemes_services/pdf/NADP/6.%20Erode%20DAP%20Final%20Report%202017-18.pdf)).

4. **Non-farm rural manufacturing / textiles / garments belt**
   Use `Chennimalai`, `Perundurai`, `Erode`, or `Gobichettipalayam`. The agriculture plan notes hosiery, readymade garments, cotton mills, and agro-processing as important industries in the district ([same source](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/govt_schemes_services/pdf/NADP/6.%20Erode%20DAP%20Final%20Report%202017-18.pdf)).

5. **Turmeric/agro-trade cluster**
   Include at least one market-linked settlement near `Erode`/`Gobichettipalayam` because the district has a major turmeric market and linked agro-processing activity ([same source](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/govt_schemes_services/pdf/NADP/6.%20Erode%20DAP%20Final%20Report%202017-18.pdf)).

6. **Forest-fringe / tribal / remote belt**
   Include `Thalavadi` or `Sathyamangalam` separately, since livelihood options, livestock dependence, wage work, and state-program dependence may differ sharply from the rest of the district.

Within each zone, choose settlements using **existing data first**:
- one higher-MGNREGA-use panchayat and one lower-use panchayat from the official MGNREGA portal ([district dashboard](https://nregastrep.nic.in/netnrega/Homedist.aspx?flag_debited=&is_statefund=&lflag=eng&district_code=2910&district_name=ERODE&state_name=TAMIL%20NADU&state_Code=29), [public data portal](https://nregarep1.nic.in/netnrega/dynamic2/dynamicreport_new4.aspx))
- one better-irrigated and one more water-stressed location using district handbooks / agriculture-plan tables
- if possible, one village and one peri-urban/town fringe site in the non-farm zone

## What to estimate from existing data instead
These are the items I would **not spend primary-survey budget on**, except maybe for validation.

| Use existing data for | Why not recollect | Best source |
|---|---|---|
| Worker composition by village/town: cultivators, agricultural laborers, household industry, other workers, main/marginal workers | Already available at village/town level; expensive to recreate and your sample will be weaker than the census for this purpose | Census 2011 PCA / DCHB for Erode ([Erode census page](https://erode.nic.in/documents/census/), [OGD PCA description](https://data.gov.in/catalog/villagetown-wise-primary-census-abstract-2011-tamil-nadu), [DCHB PDF](https://cdn.s3waas.gov.in/s3bca82e41ee7b0833588399b1fcd177c7/uploads/2018/06/2018062114.pdf)) |
| Population, households, SC/ST shares, literacy, village amenities | Same reason: already available village-wise | Census 2011 PCA / DCHB ([same sources](https://erode.nic.in/documents/census/)) |
| Number of blocks, panchayats, administrative spread | No need to survey | [Erode district administration](https://erode.nic.in/development-administration/) |
| Recent district/block totals for agriculture, irrigation, livestock, industry, banking, cooperatives | Administrative totals already compiled; better for district totals than a small household survey | [District Statistical Handbook 2023-24 page](https://erode.nic.in/document-category/statistical-report/), [Tamil Nadu DES handbook description](https://www.des.tn.gov.in/en/node/32) |
| Crop pattern, irrigation source mix, livestock concentration by block, ground-water stress categories | Good for sampling frame and zone design; no need to rebuild from scratch | [Erode District Agriculture Plan](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/govt_schemes_services/pdf/NADP/6.%20Erode%20DAP%20Final%20Report%202017-18.pdf) |
| Current MGNREGA intensity: households worked, persondays, women’s participation, 100-day households, block/GP differences | Publicly available and updated; use it as a stress/program proxy | [MGNREGA Erode dashboard](https://nregastrep.nic.in/netnrega/Homedist.aspx?flag_debited=&is_statefund=&lflag=eng&district_code=2910&district_name=ERODE&state_name=TAMIL%20NADU&state_Code=29), [MGNREGA public data portal](https://nregarep1.nic.in/netnrega/dynamic2/dynamicreport_new4.aspx) |
| Broad household infrastructure and welfare proxies such as electricity, sanitation, cooking fuel, some women’s indicators | Already available at district level; useful context, but too coarse to replace your livelihood survey | [NFHS-5 district fact sheets portal](https://rchiips.org/nfhs/districtfactsheet_NFHS-5.shtml), [DHS NFHS-5 fact sheet repository](https://dhsprogram.com/publications/publication-OF43-Other-Fact-Sheets.cfm) |

## What your primary survey should *not* try to do
Avoid spending budget on:
- a full consumption-expenditure survey
- a district-representative poverty estimate
- crop-cutting or enterprise census
- surveying all 14 blocks
- re-measuring village infrastructure already in census/administrative records

With `Rs 2 lakh`, your strongest output is:

- a **livelihood typology of Erode district**
- comparison of **6 livelihood zones**
- estimates of **current income mix, employment quality, migration, debt, and distress**
- clear identification of **which livelihoods are growing, stagnating, or acting as coping mechanisms**
