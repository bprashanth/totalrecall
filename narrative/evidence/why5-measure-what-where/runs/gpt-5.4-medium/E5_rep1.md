Q: Our NGO can survey only 300 households in Erode district this year. Which blocks should we go to and what should we ask, if the goal is understanding how farm families are coping? Citations for any data you use.

With only `300` households, I would **not** spread the sample across all `14` Erode blocks. That would leave you with about `21` households per block, which is too thin to understand coping. Instead, use a **contrast sample**: pick blocks that differ on water access, groundwater stress, and social vulnerability.

## Where To Go
I’d recommend `6` blocks with `50 households` each:

| Block | Why this block matters for a coping study | HH |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| `Talavadi` | Distinct hilly/rainfed farming system; high agricultural-labour dependence in the older district ag plan, and a large Scheduled Tribe population (`5,900` ST persons in Census 2011 PCA). This is where coping is likely to involve migration, livestock, millet/ragi, and borrowing rather than canal irrigation. [3][4] | 50 |
| `Sathyamangalam` | Mixed farming zone with **critical groundwater** in the district agriculture plan and a large ST population (`7,455`). Useful for understanding mixed crop-livestock coping and water stress. [3][4] | 50 |
| `Nambiyur` | One of the **over-exploited groundwater** blocks in the district agriculture plan. Good place to study borewell dependence, debt, input cutbacks, and crop switching. [3] | 50 |
| `Anthiyur` | Also **over-exploited groundwater**; the ag plan shows a large agricultural-labour base here. Useful for studying how families combine farming with wage labour. [3] | 50 |
| `Bhavanisagar` | Key irrigation-system block linked to the Bhavanisagar/Lower Bhavani system; the ag plan classifies it as **critical** for groundwater. Good for seeing how canal timing and reliability shape coping. [2][3][5] | 50 |
| `Kodumudi` | A canal/Cauvery-Kalingarayan farming zone and one of only **three “safe” groundwater** blocks in the ag plan. Include it as a “less water-stressed” comparison block. [3][6] | 50 |

That gives you three useful contrasts:

- `Rainfed/tribal`: `Talavadi`, `Sathyamangalam`
- `Groundwater-stressed dryland`: `Nambiyur`, `Anthiyur`
- `Canal-irrigated comparison`: `Bhavanisagar`, `Kodumudi`

If your NGO cares especially about **tail-end canal problems**, swap `Kodumudi` for `Bhavani`, since the district ag plan explicitly notes canal irrigation in `Bhavani` taluk and recent canal releases remain a live issue for farmers. [3][5][6]

## How To Sample Inside Those Blocks
Use `2 villages` per block and `25 households` per village.

In each block, pick:
- one village with **better water access** or stronger market connection
- one village that is **more water-stressed, remote, or socially vulnerable**

Within each village, try to include:
- `10` small/marginal owner-cultivators
- `5` tenant/lease-in farmers if present
- `5` farm households that rely heavily on agricultural wage labour
- `5` women-headed, SC/ST, or recent-shock households

That will tell you much more about coping than a simple random list of landowners.

## What To Ask
I’d structure the questionnaire around the **last 12 months** plus the **last 3 crop seasons**.

Ask these core questions:

1. **What changed in farming this year?**  
   Main crops in each season, acreage, fallowing, crop abandonment, delayed sowing, re-sowing.

2. **What was the water situation?**  
   Main irrigation source: canal, open well, borewell, tank, rain only. Did canal water arrive on time? Did wells/borewells fail? Did pumping cost rise?

3. **How did the family cope with water stress?**  
   Switched crops? Reduced area? Took only one crop instead of two? Shared water? Bought water? Increased mulch/drip/sprinkler?

4. **How did they change input use?**  
   Seed, fertilizer, pesticide, hired labour, machine use. What was cut first and why?

5. **What happened to yields and sales?**  
   Compare with a normal year. Did output fall because of water, pests, labour shortage, or prices?

6. **How did income sources shift?**  
   Crop income, livestock, ag wage work, non-farm work, migration, petty business, MGNREGS.

7. **Who in the family took on extra work?**  
   Men, women, youth, elderly. Did anyone migrate temporarily? Did children’s schooling or women’s unpaid work change?

8. **What happened to debt?**  
   Borrowed in the last year? From whom: bank, SHG, microfinance, gold loan, trader, input dealer, relatives. Amount, purpose, repayment stress.

9. **Were there distress sales?**  
   Livestock sold, jewellery pawned, land leased out, trees cut, equipment sold.

10. **Was food consumption affected?**  
    Fewer meals? Less diverse diet? Reduced milk/eggs/pulses? Borrowing food?

11. **Did they receive any support that actually helped?**  
    Crop insurance, input subsidy, micro-irrigation support, extension advice, PMFBY claim, fodder support, SHG loan, PDS, pensions, MGNREGS.  
    This matters in Erode because the district agriculture department highlights irrigation support, rainfed integrated farming, mechanization, and PMFBY-notified crops such as maize, groundnut, redgram, ragi, sesame, samba paddy, and sugarcane. [2]

12. **What is their plan for next year?**  
    Continue farming? Reduce acreage? Shift to livestock? Shift to millets/maize/groundnut? Leave land idle? Stop farming?

Two open-ended questions are especially valuable:
- “What was the hardest month for your family in the last year, and why?”
- “What is the one thing that would most improve your ability to cope next year?”

## One Practical Note
The official sources that best differentiate blocks are partly older block-level publications, especially the `2011 Census` and the `2017-18` district agriculture plan. They are still useful for **choosing contrasting block types**, but I would use your survey, not those reports, to estimate current coping prevalence. [3][4]
