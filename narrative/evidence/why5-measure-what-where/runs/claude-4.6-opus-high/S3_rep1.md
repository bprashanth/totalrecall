Q: Between Erode town and Gobichettipalayam, which one has grown more since 2015 going by satellite data? Citations please.
elapsed:55s

---

Based on the available satellite-derived evidence, **Erode town has grown significantly more than Gobichettipalayam since 2015**.

Here is what the satellite data shows:

## Erode Town — Documented Rapid Growth

- **Built-up area** increased from 23.5 km² (2013) to 27.75 km² (2018), a **5.48% increase** in just five years, with a predicted expansion to ~39.6% urbanization by 2023. The study used high-resolution geospatial datasets and GIS-based delineation of built-up area.
- **Municipal boundary expansion**: The Erode City Municipal Corporation's jurisdiction grew from 8.35 km² to 109.52 km² (pre-2024), and was further expanded to **138.98 km²** in January 2025 by merging four adjacent village panchayats — a direct response to "population influx, commercial expansion, and industrial activity."
- Erode is classified as a **Tier-2 city** under the Smart City Mission and falls in the "western corridor" of Tamil Nadu, which VIIRS nighttime light data identifies as having **more urban growth than other corridors** in the state.

## Gobichettipalayam — Comparatively Modest Growth

- No dedicated satellite-based urban sprawl study exists for Gobichettipalayam in the published literature.
- It remains a **municipality** (not a corporation), described as "bi-functional" with 31% workforce in agriculture. Its economy is still "predominantly dependent on agriculture."
- VIIRS nightlight studies note that "smaller cities showed minimal change" in night light intensity between 2015–2023, while growth was concentrated in Tier-1 and Tier-2 hubs like Erode.

## Regional Context (Kongu Nadu LULC Study, 2017–2024)

The broader Kongu Nadu region (which includes both towns) saw built-up area increase from 4,741.61 km² to 6,497.79 km² between 2017 and 2024 — but this growth is dominated by larger industrial centers like Erode, Coimbatore, and Tiruppur.

**Bottom line**: Every available satellite-derived indicator — built-up area classification, VIIRS nighttime light intensity, and administrative boundary expansions driven by observed growth — points to Erode town having grown substantially more than Gobichettipalayam since 2015. Gobichettipalayam remains a smaller agricultural municipality with no comparable documented expansion in the remote sensing literature.
