# Survey Design for Farm Family Coping in Erode District

## Recommended Blocks for Sampling

Based on agro-ecological diversity and vulnerability, I'd recommend focusing on **5-6 blocks** that capture the range of farming conditions in Erode district:

### Priority Blocks (high agricultural vulnerability)

1. **Gobichettipalayam** – Major paddy and sugarcane area dependent on Bhavani river/LBP canal; vulnerable to water release failures
2. **Sathyamangalam** – Rainfed dryland farming (millets, pulses, cotton) at the forest fringe; wildlife conflict and erratic rainfall
3. **Nambiyur** – Dryland block with turmeric and groundnut; high dependence on borewells with declining water tables
4. **Thalavadi** – Tribal-dominated, hilly terrain; subsistence farming with limited market access

### Complementary Blocks (for comparison)

5. **Bhavani** – Peri-urban block with diversified livelihoods; mix of irrigated and rain-fed agriculture
6. **Perundurai** – Industrial-agricultural interface; captures non-farm income diversification

### Sampling Allocation (n=300)

| Block | Households | Rationale |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Gobichettipalayam | 60 | Canal-irrigated, commercial crops |
| Sathyamangalam | 60 | Rainfed, forest-fringe, tribal areas |
| Nambiyur | 50 | Groundwater-stressed dryland |
| Thalavadi | 50 | Remote tribal subsistence farming |
| Bhavani | 40 | Peri-urban diversification |
| Perundurai | 40 | Industrial corridor comparison |

Use **multi-stage cluster sampling**: randomly select 3-4 village panchayats per block, then 12-15 households per village using systematic random sampling from voter/ration card lists.

## What to Ask: Core Survey Modules

### Module 1: Household & Farm Profile
- Household size, dependency ratio, social category (SC/ST/OBC)
- Total landholding (owned + leased), irrigation source
- Primary and secondary crops (last 2 seasons)
- Livestock ownership

### Module 2: Shocks & Stressors (last 3 years)
- Drought/deficit rainfall episodes experienced
- Crop loss events (pest, flood, unseasonal rain)
- Input cost spikes (fertiliser, diesel, labour)
- Market price crashes for key crops
- Health shocks requiring large expenditure
- Human-wildlife conflict (especially in Sathyamangalam/Thalavadi)

### Module 3: Coping Strategies (adapted from Maxwell Coping Strategies Index)
- **Erosive coping**: Sold productive assets, took high-interest loans, reduced food intake, pulled children from school, migrated for distress labour
- **Non-erosive coping**: Switched crops, reduced cultivated area, sought MGNREGA work, used savings, received family transfers
- **Adaptive strategies**: Adopted drip/micro-irrigation, diversified to livestock/poultry, joined FPOs, took crop insurance (PMFBY)

### Module 4: Institutional Access
- Access to crop insurance (enrolled? claimed? received payout?)
- MGNREGA days worked in last year
- KCC/crop loan status and indebtedness
- Contact with agricultural extension (KVK Erode, state AO)
- SHG/FPO membership
- PDS and food security entitlements

### Module 5: Subjective Well-being & Future Outlook
- Self-rated food security (past 30 days)
- Perceived ability to withstand another bad season (Likert scale)
- Willingness to continue farming vs. exit intention
- Priority needs (open-ended)

## Practical Tips

- **Pilot** in one village in Gobichettipalayam before full deployment
- Conduct during **January-February** (post-Samba harvest) when farmers are available and recall of the season is fresh
- Use **Tamil-language** instruments; train enumerators from local colleges (Erode Arts College, Govt. Arts College Sathyamangalam)
- Budget for **2 revisits** per sampled household to reduce non-response bias
- Pair with **5-6 focus group discussions** (one per block, mixed gender) for qualitative depth on coping narratives

This design balances agro-ecological diversity, vulnerability coverage, and logistical feasibility within your 300-household constraint.
