For India's 2021 unemployment rate, here are the three estimates, their methodologies, and a recommendation for a district NGO report.

---

### 1. ILO Modelled Estimate (World Bank)
* **Figure (2021)**: **5.98%** (in the latest World Bank WDI database updates, e.g., Jan 2025) or **6.38%** (in earlier database releases).
* **Methodology**: Produced by the International Labour Organization (ILO) to ensure international comparability. It harmonizes national labor force surveys (like the PLFS) and applies econometric models to impute missing data, adjust for definition/coverage differences, and smooth out short-term volatility. It defines the unemployed as those aged 15+ without work, available for work, and actively seeking employment in a brief reference period.
* **Citation**: World Bank, World Development Indicators (WDI), Indicator Code: `SL.UEM.TOTL.ZS` (updated Jan 2025).

### 2. Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)
Because the official PLFS annual reports are released in July–June cycles, the figures spanning 2021 are:
* **PLFS 2020–21 (July 2020 – June 2021)**:
  * **Usual Status (US)**: **4.2%** (for persons aged 15 and above)
  * **Current Weekly Status (CWS)**: **7.5%** (for persons aged 15 and above)
* **PLFS 2021–22 (July 2021 – June 2022)**:
  * **Usual Status (US)**: **4.1%** (for persons aged 15 and above)
  * **Current Weekly Status (CWS)**: **6.6%** (for persons aged 15 and above)
* **Methodology**:
  * **Usual Status (US)**: Measures activity over a **365-day** reference period. A person is employed if they worked for a relatively long part of the year (principal status) or at least 30 days (subsidiary status). This captures chronic, long-term structural unemployment.
  * **Current Weekly Status (CWS)**: Measures activity over a **7-day** reference period. A person is employed if they worked for at least 1 hour on any day during the reference week. This is highly sensitive to short-term, casual, and seasonal employment dynamics.
* **Citation**: National Sample Survey Office (NSSO), Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI), Government of India, *Annual Report, Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)* (2020-21 & 2021-22).

### 3. CMIE Estimate
* **Figure (2021)**: **7.8%** (average of the 12 monthly rates for calendar year 2021, which fluctuated from a low of 6.50% in March 2021 to a peak of 11.84% in May 2021 due to the second wave of the pandemic).
* **Methodology**: Conducted by the Centre for Monitoring Indian Economy (CMIE) via the Consumer Pyramids Household Survey (CPHS), a continuous, high-frequency survey of over 170,000 households. It uses a **highly stringent definition** of employment: a person is employed only if they worked for the better part of the day of the survey or the day before. It does not count marginal, casual, or very low-hour work (like working 1 hour a week) as employment, resulting in a higher reported unemployment rate than PLFS.
* **Citation**: Centre for Monitoring Indian Economy (CMIE), *Consumer Pyramids Household Survey (CPHS)*, Monthly Unemployment Rate database (2021).

### Which one should a district NGO report use?

A district NGO report should use the **PLFS (Periodic Labour Force Survey) figures** (specifically focusing on both Usual Status and Current Weekly Status):

1. **Official Credibility**: PLFS is the official government benchmark. Using it ensures the NGO’s report is credible and comparable to government policy documents, district census handbooks, and state economic surveys.
2. **Structural vs. Seasonal Insight**: 
   * **Usual Status** helps the NGO highlight chronic, long-term unemployment.
   * **Current Weekly Status (CWS)** is critical for rural/semi-urban districts because it captures seasonal unemployment, underemployment, and casual labor fluctuations—vital for local NGO planning (e.g., MGNREGA demand, skill training, or drought relief).
3. **Granularity**: While the ILO estimate is purely macro-national and CMIE is state-level, PLFS microdata can sometimes be filtered to the district level or contrasted with state-level averages to provide local context.
