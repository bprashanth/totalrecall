Q: Garbage complaints in Bellandur ward, Bengaluru: from 2019 to 2022 did they go up or come down? Citations.
elapsed:284s

---

To understand the trend of garbage complaints in Bellandur ward (Ward 150), Bengaluru, from 2019 to 2022, we analyzed two primary datasets hosted on the OpenCity urban data portal: **Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) Grievances (Sahaaya) Data** and **Janaagraha’s IChangeMyCity (iCMyC) Complaints Log**. 

The trend depends on the platform analyzed, showing a shift in how citizens reported grievances during this period:

---

### 1. Official BBMP Grievances (Sahaaya) Portal
On the official BBMP grievance platform, garbage-related complaints **went up** steadily from 2020 to 2022. 

* **2020**: **352** strict garbage complaints *(out of 1,540 total ward complaints)*
* **2021**: **384** strict garbage complaints *(out of 1,480 total ward complaints)*
* **2022**: **443** strict garbage complaints *(out of 2,207 total ward complaints)*

This represents a **25.8% increase** in strict garbage complaints over two years. 

*(Note: If we include broader waste-induced issues like unhygienic commercial premises and mosquito density, the numbers rose from **448** in 2020 to **492** in 2022).*

---

### 2. Janaagraha’s IChangeMyCity Portal
On this third-party civic portal, garbage-related complaints **came down** significantly.

* **2019**: **29** complaints
* **2020**: **16** complaints
* **2021**: **6** complaints
* **2022**: **4** complaints

**Why the divergence?** This decline is a platform-specific artifact. In early 2020, the BBMP upgraded and heavily promoted its official grievance app, **Sahaaya 2.0 (Namma Bengaluru)**. Complainants migrated away from third-party portals like IChangeMyCity to report issues directly to the BBMP.

---

### 3. Ground Reality & Context
Local news reports and citizen action groups (such as *Kasa Muktha Bellandur*) confirm that garbage issues on the ground actually **escalated** during this period:
* **Infrastructure Gaps:** Bellandur's population surged past 80,000, but its two Dry Waste Collection Centers (DWCCs) remained dilapidated and under-capacitated, forcing waste into drains and vacant plots.
* **Garbage Burning:** Open burning of unsegregated trash by unauthorized collectors became a daily hazard near Bellandur Lake and Kasavanahalli, sparking viral social media outcries and fire department interventions.
* **Citizen Interventions:** In late 2022, after three years of continuous follow-up, residents successfully bypassed BBMP delays by using CSR funds to install a "Kasa Kiosk" on Kasavanahalli Main Road to eliminate a major chronic blackspot.
